## Scenario

The Department of Data Science holds its results meeting on Monday. The examinations office has sent a
CSV of marks, but it was assembled by hand: some scores are text, one student appears twice, and two
courses have different numbers of students. The coordinator, Dr. Saima, has one hour to prepare a
per-course summary she can defend to the board. She does not want a model — she wants numbers she can
quote, and a figure for the slide.

## Your role

You are the teaching assistant preparing that summary. You report to the course coordinator and you will
be judged on **correctness and clarity**: a wrong mean that looks authoritative is worse than no mean at
all.

## What you are given

| Item | Location | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Raw marks | generated by the script in Task 1 | 5 columns, ~120 rows, deliberate faults |
| Starter code | none | write it yourself; the lessons cover every operation |
| Constraints | — | standard library, NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib only; no manual edits to the data |

**Not provided:** a cleaned dataset, a reference solution, or the correct per-course figures. You must
derive them, and be able to show how.

## Requirements

**Functional**

- R1 — Load the raw records and convert every score column to numeric, reporting how many values could
  not be converted.
- R2 — Remove duplicate students by `student_id`, keeping the record with the higher total, and report
  how many were removed.
- R3 — For each course, report the count, mean, median and standard deviation of scores.
- R4 — Produce one figure with a histogram of all scores and a boxplot of scores by course.
- R5 — Handle a course with fewer than three students without crashing, and mark it as unreliable in
  the output.

**Non-functional**

- N1 — The whole script runs in under 10 seconds on a laptop CPU.
- N2 — `np.random.default_rng(seed=2026)` produces the same data on every run.
- N3 — Every number in your report is printed by your code, not typed by hand.

## Tasks

1. **Generate the raw data.** Create a DataFrame with `student_id`, `student_name`, `course`, `score`
   and `attendance_pct`. Use 120 rows, three courses, scores from a normal distribution with different
   means per course, then deliberately corrupt it: store 8 scores as text (`"78"` and one `"n/a"`),
   duplicate two students, and leave three scores empty. *(success: `df.shape` is `(120, 5)` and
   `df.select_dtypes("number").columns` is shorter than you expect)*
2. **Inspect before touching.** Print shape, the numeric columns, the missing count per column, and the
   five rows with the highest score. *(success: you can state which columns are text and why)*
3. **Clean.** Convert `score` with `pd.to_numeric(..., errors="coerce")`, record the number of
   non-convertible values, drop exact duplicate rows, then keep one row per `student_id` using the higher
   total score. *(success: no duplicated `student_id` remains and your cleaning report lists what was
   removed)*
4. **Summarise.** Group by course and compute count, mean, median and standard deviation, sorted by
   mean. *(success: exactly one row per course, with a count that sums to the cleaned row count)*
5. **Plot.** One figure, two panels: histogram of all scores with 15 bins, and a boxplot of score by
   course. Label both axes and give the figure a title naming the term. *(success: the figure is
   readable without the surrounding text)*
6. **Report.** Print a five-line summary for Dr. Saima: number of students, number of records removed,
   best course by median, worst course by median, and one sentence naming a limitation of the data.

## Extension challenges

1. **Per-course attendance.** Add mean attendance per course and check whether it tracks the mean score;
   report the correlation with `df["attendance_pct"].corr(df["score"])`.
2. **Missing-data sensitivity.** Recompute the course means with the missing scores imputed by the course
   median, and report how much each mean moves.
3. **Scale.** Rewrite the summary using only NumPy (no pandas) and compare the line count.

## Hints

<details><summary>Hint 1 — getting started</summary>

Build the honest frame first, then corrupt a copy of it. Keeping the clean version in a separate
variable lets you check your cleaning step against a known answer.

</details>

<details><summary>Hint 2 — the likely sticking point</summary>

`groupby(...).agg(["count", "mean", "median", "std"])` returns a DataFrame with a MultiIndex column
header. Use `.round(2)` and `.to_string()` to print it readably, and do not be surprised that the column
names are tuples.

</details>

<details><summary>Hint 3 — checking your work</summary>

The per-course counts must sum to the cleaned row count, and each course mean must lie between that
course's minimum and maximum score. If either check fails, the cleaning step is the suspect.

</details>

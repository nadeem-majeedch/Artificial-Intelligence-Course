## Part A — Applications and hybrid systems (1–4)

**1.** The eligibility demo's rules-only model scored 0.972 and the neural model 0.877. Give the
honest interpretation, and say why the hybrid (0.931) is still the deployable design.

**2.** The model approved 100 policy-ineligible applicants. Why is that a different kind of
failure from an accuracy drop?

**3.** Integrated gradients put `savings` first (+0.457) and `income` last (+0.035), while the
binding rule was the income cap. What does this show about attribution as an explanation?

**4.** Name the dominant failure mode for applied systems in health and in agriculture, and say
why they differ.

## Part B — Serving and efficiency (5–8)

**5.** Distillation gained −0.004 over training without a teacher. Give two conditions under
which distillation does help.

**6.** int8 reduced size to 27.7% and made inference 1.6–2.4× slower in release validation. Explain
the mechanism and name two conditions under which it would be faster.

**7.** Pruning 50% of weights left size at 100% and changed latency by 0.8–1.3×, with no reliable
speed-up. What has to change for pruning to pay?

**8.** Why is the parameter count (74) misleading for the quantised model?

## Part C — Monitoring, multimodal and graphs (9–12)

**9.** Boosting had the narrowest prediction intervals and 0.66 coverage against a nominal 0.80.
Why is that worse than a wider interval with correct coverage?

**10.** PSI reached 3.777 while MAE had not yet moved much. Explain why input monitoring is the
early-warning system.

**11.** Zero-shot digit accuracy reached 0.904 while instance-level image-to-text R@1 stayed at
0.024. Give the correct interpretation and the metric you would report.

**12.** The GCN beat the MLP by 0.467 at homophily 0.773, but lost by 0.131 at 0.187; a second
family (SBM) showed the same pattern (−0.120 to +0.395). State the design rule this implies, and
say why the 0.906 measured at perfect homophily is not a reason to skip measuring homophily.
Also state whether the advantage survived the inductive split, and which part of the protocol
that claim depends on.

## Part D — Federated learning and research practice (13–15)

**13.** FedAvg reached 0.898 against 0.917 centralised, from clients scoring 0.176 alone. What
does the per-class spread of 0.364 add to that summary?

**14.** A system claims differential privacy with clipping 1.0 and sigma 0.02. What is missing,
and why does it matter?

**15.** Name the four kinds of research claim and the evidence each requires.

## Answer key

**1.** The labels are generated by the policy plus 3% noise, so the rule is the true function and
the model learns noise. The hybrid is deployable because it guarantees zero policy violations —
a property the rules-only model has too, but the hybrid keeps a learned component for cases the
rule cannot express (in a real setting) and routes them to review.

**2.** An accuracy drop is a statistical statement about the model. A policy violation is a
wrongful decision about a person, with legal and financial consequences, that no average
captures. Compliance is a constraint, not a metric.

**3.** Attribution ranks sensitivity of the model's output; the reason for a decision is the
binding constraint. They can diverge completely, which is why adverse-action reasons must come
from the rule or a counterfactual, not from a saliency map.

**4.** Health: prevalence shift and subgroup error, because the clinic population differs from
the trial. Agriculture: distribution shift in field conditions (lighting, occlusion, novel
pathogens). Health's risk is statistical and regulatory; agriculture's is perceptual and
environmental.

**5.** When the student is capacity-limited relative to the task, and when soft targets carry
structure the hard label hides (class similarity, label noise, an ensemble teacher).

**6.** Weights are stored int8 but dequantised to float on every forward pass, so the conversion
cost can exceed the arithmetic saved. In release validation the ratio ranged from 1.61× to 2.35×
across five runs (median 1.68×). It is faster where int8 kernels exist (mobile NPUs, quantised
inference engines) or where memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic dominates.

**7.** A sparse kernel, a sparse storage format, or structured pruning that yields a genuinely
smaller dense matrix. Otherwise the zeros are still multiplied and still stored.

**8.** Int8 weights are registered as buffers, not parameters, so the parameter count reflects
only biases. Bytes are the meaningful measure.

**9.** Coverage is the property people rely on: an 80% interval that contains the truth 66% of
the time systematically understates risk. A wide interval is honest; a too-narrow one is a false
claim.

**10.** Labels arrive late, so error metrics lag by a full window. Input distributions are
observable immediately, and a change in them is a necessary condition for the model to
misbehave. PSI fires before MAE because it does not need labels.

**11.** The model learned the alignment; the metric was ill-posed, because many captions describe
the same digit, so exact-pair retrieval is near-impossible by construction. Report digit-level
retrieval (0.961), and state the instance-level number only with its chance level.

**12.** Measure homophily before choosing a graph architecture. Below roughly 0.4, message
passing mixes classes and a feature-only model is better. The advantage did survive the strict
inductive split (+0.575 random-mix, +0.465 SBM), but that claim depends on test-node features and
test-to-training edges being available at inference, and on validation-only epoch selection. The
0.906 at perfect homophily is an upper bound for a graph where every edge is same-class, not a
general result.

**13.** It shows the average hides who paid. With a 0.36 spread, some classes (and therefore some
clients) are much worse off; the global metric is not a fairness statement.

**14.** The privacy budget: no epsilon, no accounting of composition across rounds and sampling.
Without it, "differentially private" is a description of the mechanism, not a guarantee about
disclosure.

**15.** Existence (a worked example or benchmark), comparative (same data, budget, seeds,
intervals), mechanistic (an ablation isolating the cause), generality (multiple datasets,
including one that fails).
